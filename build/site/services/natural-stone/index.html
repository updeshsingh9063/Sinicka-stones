Stone is a natural material. It behaves like one.

Marble, limestone, travertine and onyx installed across London, specified, sourced, set out and sealed by NVQ Level 2 certified installers.

Get a Quote

Every slab is different, and that is the point.

Natural stone is quarried, not manufactured.

Veining, shade and porosity vary between blocks and even between slabs cut from the same block. That variation is the reason people specify stone, and the reason it needs handling differently from porcelain.

It means slabs are selected and sequenced rather than pulled from a pallet at random. It means the vein direction is decided before anything is cut, so a floor reads as a considered surface rather than an assortment. And it means the material is sealed appropriately, because most natural stone is porous and will mark if it is not protected.

Marble stains and etches. Limestone is soft and absorbent. Travertine has natural voids that may need filling. None of these are faults. They are properties, and the specification has to account for them.

Sealing is a specification

Not an afterthought, and not a final-day extra.

An impregnating sealer soaks into the stone and resists staining without changing the appearance; a topical sealer sits on the surface and alters the finish. The right choice depends on the stone, the finish and where it is going: a honed limestone hallway and a polished marble bathroom need different answers.

What we install.

  • Marble

    Floors, walls, bathrooms and feature elevations, with vein direction and slab sequencing decided before cutting.

  • Limestone

    Honed and brushed limestone floors, sealed appropriately for a soft, absorbent stone.

  • Travertine

    Filled or unfilled, vein-cut or cross-cut, with the void treatment agreed up front.

  • Onyx

    Translucent stone including backlit applications, where the substrate and fixing method have to be considered as part of the effect.

  • Book-matched slabs

    Mirrored veining across a wall, bath panel or feature so the pattern reads as a single piece.

  • Restoration & honing

    Grinding, honing and re-polishing existing stone floors that have dulled or scratched.

How a stone floor is set out.

The order matters. Once stone is cut, the decisions in it are permanent.

  1. 01Slabs viewed and selected at the supplier where possible
  2. 02Vein direction and sequencing agreed before cutting
  3. 03Substrate levelled; uncoupling layer where movement is expected
  4. 04Dry-lay to confirm sequencing, cuts and shade run
  5. 05Fixed with the adhesive appropriate to the stone and its thickness
  6. 06Sealed before grouting where the stone is porous, then again on completion

Natural stone.

All questions
Will marble stain in a bathroom?

Marble is porous and reactive. It can stain and it can etch from acidic products. Properly sealed and sensibly maintained it performs well in bathrooms, and many people accept a degree of patina as part of the material. If you want a surface that will not change at all, we will say so and suggest porcelain instead.

How much variation should I expect between slabs?

That depends entirely on the stone. Some limestones are very consistent; some marbles vary dramatically. Wherever possible we view and select the actual slabs, and we always dry-lay before fixing so you can see the run before it is permanent.

Can natural stone go over underfloor heating?

Yes, with the right build-up. Stone conducts heat well, which makes it a good pairing, but the system needs an uncoupling layer and a proper commissioning sequence so the floor is not heated before the adhesive has cured.

How often does stone need resealing?

It varies with the stone, the sealer and the traffic. We will tell you what was applied and give aftercare guidance at handover rather than leaving you to guess.
