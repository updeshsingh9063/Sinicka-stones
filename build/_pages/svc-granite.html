Density is the whole argument.

Granite is an igneous stone, significantly harder and less porous than marble or limestone.

It resists scratching, staining and acid etching in a way softer stones do not, which is why it ends up in entrance halls, commercial lobbies and kitchens.

That hardness cuts both ways. Granite is slower to cut and harder on tooling, and the tolerances are unforgiving. A mitre that is fractionally out cannot be eased into place. Cutting is planned and measured rather than adjusted on site.

For a floor that has to look the same in fifteen years as it does on handover, in a space that takes real traffic, granite is usually the right specification.

Where granite earns its cost

Where the surface takes punishment.

In a low-traffic bathroom, a softer stone or a porcelain will look just as good for less. Granite pays for itself in building entrances, commercial circulation, kitchen floors and external thresholds. We will say when a project does not need it.

All questions
Is granite better than marble?

Not better, different. Granite is harder, denser and far more resistant to staining and etching. Marble offers veining and a depth of colour granite rarely matches. Choose granite for durability, marble for appearance, and be clear which matters more in that particular room.

Does granite need sealing?

Less often than marble or limestone, and some granites barely need it at all. It depends on the specific stone and its finish, so we test absorption rather than applying a blanket rule.

Is polished granite slippery underfoot?

Polished granite can be slippery when wet, so for entrances, bathrooms and external thresholds we would normally specify a honed or flamed finish instead. Slip resistance is a specification decision, not something to discover after installation.

Can granite be used outside?

Yes. It is one of the better stones for external use, given a frost-resistant selection and an appropriate finish and fixing method for the exposure.
