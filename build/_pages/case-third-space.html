The challenge

A wet environment that never closes.

A health club is one of the most demanding environments a tiled surface can face.

Changing rooms, wet rooms and a pool surround are wet for most of the day, every day, with heavy footfall across the whole floor plate and cleaning regimes that are harder on grout and sealant than any domestic use.

Three requirements had to hold at once: the waterproofing had to be genuinely continuous across a large and complex floor area; the surface had to meet slip resistance where people walk barefoot and wet; and the finish had to look like a premium facility rather than a utilitarian one.

Waterproof first, then set out.

The tanking system was installed as a continuous layer across walls and floors, with the manufacturer’s own banding at every internal corner, pipe penetration and drain upstand, sealed into the membrane rather than dressed around it.

Only once the waterproof layer was complete and checked did setting out begin. Falls to drainage were formed in the substrate, and the tile module was set out so that cuts landed away from sightlines and the pattern ran cleanly through door openings between adjoining areas.

Materials

Large-format porcelain.

Porcelain was specified over natural stone for a wet, high-traffic, heavily-cleaned commercial environment. It is effectively non-porous, dimensionally consistent across a large floor plate, and unaffected by the cleaning chemicals a leisure facility uses daily.

Large formats were chosen deliberately: fewer joints means less grout, and grout is the component that discolours, degrades and needs maintaining. Slip resistance was specified for each zone rather than applied uniformly. Barefoot wet areas do not have the same requirement as a dry changing area.
